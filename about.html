    About Jainism

    Know Jainism

    Tap a question to read its answer.

    Jainism is one of the oldest living religions. It is a path to a peaceful life and, in the end, to freedom of the soul. It teaches non-violence, truth and self-control.

    Mahavir was the 24th Tirthankara, born around 599 BCE in Bihar. He left his royal life, practised deep meditation for twelve years and then taught how to live without harm.

    Tirthankaras are great teachers who reached liberation and showed others the path. Jains honour 24 of them in this era, from Rishabhanatha to Mahavira. See all of them, with their emblems (chinha), in the cards below.

    Every soul is equal and can become free. Our own actions, not a creator, shape our future.

    To end suffering by purifying the soul, and to live so that no being is hurt by us.

    Non-violence is the first rule. Avoid unnecessary harm, control anger, and respect every living being.

    Truth has many sides. Listen to other views before you judge, and you will learn to respect people who think differently.

    Reduce possessions and wants. Share with others and help those in need.

    Every action leaves a mark on the soul. Good, kind actions lighten it; anger and greed make it heavy.

    Liberation: the soul becomes free of all karma and rests in pure knowledge and bliss.

    Samyak Darshan (right faith), Samyak Gyan (right knowledge) and Samyak Charitra (right conduct). Together they form the path to liberation.

    The 24 Tirthankaras

    Tirthankaras (“ford-makers”) crossed the ocean of rebirth and showed others the way. Each has a chinha, a special emblem that helps devotees recognise their idols. A few emblems and details differ between the Shvetambar and Digambar traditions.
